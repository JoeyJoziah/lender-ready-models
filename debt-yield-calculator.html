DSCR binds, so the loan is $3,934,000 after rounding down to the nearest $1,000. On that loan the LTV is 61.5%, the DSCR is 1.25x and the debt yield is 9.73%.

The debt yield test is the one a lower rate or a longer amortization cannot loosen. If the rate fell far enough for the DSCR loan to reach the $4,480,000 LTV cap, the debt yield on that loan would be 8.54%, just above the 8.5% floor; a little lower and debt yield becomes the limit. Questions

What is debt yield?

Debt yield is net operating income divided by the loan amount. It is the return a lender would earn on its loan balance if it took the property back on day one. A $382,764 NOI on a $3,934,000 loan is a 9.73% debt yield.

How do you calculate the maximum loan from a minimum debt yield?

Divide NOI by the minimum debt yield. With $382,764 of NOI and an 8.5% minimum, the debt yield test allows at most $4,503,100. The lender then compares that figure with the LTV and DSCR limits and lends the smallest of the three.

How is debt yield different from DSCR?

DSCR compares NOI with the annual mortgage payment, so it moves with the interest rate and amortization. Debt yield compares NOI with the loan balance, so the rate, the amortization and the appraisal have no effect on it. Only more NOI raises the debt yield loan.

What minimum debt yield do lenders require?

It is set by the lender's program and varies by property type, market and loan type. Use the figure on your term sheet. The 8.5% default in this calculator is the template's example input, not a market standard.

Is this calculator advice?

No. It is simplified arithmetic. It does not include interest-only periods, loan rounding rules, reserves or the lender's own NOI adjustments, all of which change the answer.
